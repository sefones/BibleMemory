Bible Memory Skills — Documentation

A quick guide to memorizing Bible verses with Verse Card.

1. Getting Started

  1. Open the app. There's nothing to set up first — go straight to adding or practicing verses. You'll pick a version each time you add a verse.
  2. Add a verse using the Book, Chapter, Verse, and Through verse dropdowns (the last one is only needed for a verse range). Then type, paste, or import the verse text yourself — see "Adding a Verse" below.
  3. Choose how you want to practice:
    • Random verse — the app picks one from your saved list for you.
    • Specific verse — pick one from your saved library.

Adding a Verse

  • Book / Chapter / Verse / Through verse: pick from the dropdowns — they only ever show valid combinations, and "Through verse" lets you save a range (e.g. John 3:16-18).
  • Version: pick from the list, or choose Other and type a custom name.
  • Verse text: type it, paste it, dictate it (in browsers that support it), or import it from a file.
  • Paste straight from Bible.com: see the next section. Pasting the copied verse fills in the book, chapter, verse, version, and text for you.

Pasting a verse copied from Bible.com

Copy a verse on Bible.com (the copy includes the verse, the reference, and a link), then paste it into the Verse text box on the Add Verse screen. The app reads it and fills in the form for you:

'“How can someone be born when they are old?” Nicodemus asked. “Surely they cannot enter a second time into their mother’s womb to be born!” '

John 3:4
https://www.bible.com/bible/111/JHN.3.4.NIV
  • Book, Chapter, Verse: set from the reference (for example John 3:4). The Bible.com link is also used, so books such as Psalms and 1 John are matched correctly.
  • Version: read from the end of the link (NIV in the example). If it is not in the version list, it is filled in under Other.
  • Verse text: only the verse itself is kept. The link and the reference are never included, and the verse's own quotation marks are left as they are.
  • Check the result against your Bible, then tap Save verse.
  • If you paste several verses copied from Bible.com at once, they are all saved to your library right away (verses you already have are skipped).
  • Pasting a verse and reference without the link also works, as long as the reference is on its own line (for example John 3:16 NIV).

Bulk-import format

The importer accepts the direct Bible.com copy/paste format. This is the preferred format because it keeps the verse text, reference, and translation link together:

'For God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son, that whoever believes in him shall not perish but have eternal life. '

John 3:16
[https://www.bible.com/bible/111/JHN.3.16.NIV](https://www.bible.com/bible/111/JHN.3.16.NIV)

For a range, the reference and link contain the full range:

'For God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son, that whoever believes in him shall not perish but have eternal life. For God did not send his Son into the world to condemn the world, but to save the world through him. '

John 3:16-17
[https://www.bible.com/bible/111/JHN.3.16-17.NIV](https://www.bible.com/bible/111/JHN.3.16-17.NIV)

KJV and other translations work the same way:

'For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life. For God sent not his Son into the world to condemn the world; but that the world through him might be saved. '

John 3:16-17
[https://www.bible.com/bible/1/JHN.3.16-17.KJV](https://www.bible.com/bible/1/JHN.3.16-17.KJV)

Important: each entry is separated by a blank line. The importer reads the version automatically from the final part of the Bible.com link — NIV, KJV, CEB, etc. You do not need to manually add | NIV to the reference.

How the Bible.com import is interpreted

  1. Verse text: the copied verse becomes the saved verse text. If Bible.com wrapped it in straight quotation marks (such as '...'), that outer wrapper is removed. Quotation marks that are part of the verse itself, such as “How can someone be born...”, are kept.
  2. Reference: the Bible.com link supplies the Book / Chapter / Verse (for example JHN.13.34 is John 13:34). The reference line, such as John 13:34 or John 13:34-35, is used if there is no link.
  3. Version: the final code in the Bible.com URL is used for the translation, such as ESV, NIV, or KJV. The URL itself is ignored and is never included in the saved verse text.
  4. Verse ranges: if the reference is hyphenated, such as John 13:34-35, the importer tries to divide the copied text into the same number of verses using clear sentence-ending punctuation, normally periods. When it can match the number of pieces to the number of verses, it saves each verse separately as John 13:34, John 13:35, etc.
  5. Blank line: separates one imported entry from the next.

Example:

'A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another: just as I have loved you, you also are to love one another. '

John 13:34
https://www.bible.com/bible/59/JHN.13.34.ESV

This imports as John 13:34, version ESV, with only the quoted sentence as the verse text.

Older bulk format

The original Reference | Version format is still supported. This is useful for existing exports and manually prepared files:

Jeremiah 17:9-10 | NIV

'The heart is deceitful above all things and beyond cure. Who can understand it? “I the Lord search the heart and examine the mind, to reward each person according to their conduct, according to what their deeds deserve.” '

For a hyphenated range in this older format, the importer uses the same range handling as Bible.com imports and creates separate verse entries when the text can be divided into the matching number of verse pieces.

Existing exports from older versions are still accepted, so you do not need to recreate your existing library files:

John 3:16 | NIV
For God so loved the world...

Romans 8:28 | NIV
And we know that for those who love God...

Import this verse (.txt) is still available for a single text-only verse. Bulk-import (.txt) should be used when importing the Bible.com examples above or several verses at once.

Sharing Your Library (Share / Export / Import)

Share your verses with someone

  • Tap 📤 Share on the home screen, above your saved verses. Your phone's share menu opens so you can send all of your saved verses through Messages, WhatsApp, email, AirDrop, or any other app.
  • Where possible the verses are attached as a .txt file. If an app can't take a file, they are sent as plain text instead.
  • On a computer (or anywhere the share menu isn't available), Share copies your verses so you can paste them into a message or email.

Receiving verses someone shared with you

  • If you got a file: save it, then tap Import on the home screen and choose the file.
  • If you got the verses as text: copy the whole message, tap Add a verse, and paste it into the Verse text box. Every verse in the message is added to your library at once.
  • Verses you already have (same reference, version, and text) are skipped automatically, so it's safe to import the same verses more than once.

Export and Import (backup)

  • Export (on the home screen, above your saved verses) downloads a .txt file of every verse you've saved, in the same format bulk-import reads — reference and version on one line, the text on the next, verses separated by a blank line.
  • Send that file to someone else, or keep it as a backup.
  • Import reads an export file (yours or someone else's) and adds any verses that aren't already in your library — exact duplicates (same reference, version, and text) are skipped automatically, so it's safe to import the same file more than once.

Add Verse Card to Your Phone or Tablet

Once Verse Card is hosted at a web address (https), you can put it on your home screen like a normal app, with its own icon.

  • iPhone / iPad: open the link in Safari, tap Share, choose Add to Home Screen, then tap Add.
  • Android: open the link in Chrome, tap the ⋮ menu, then choose Install app or Add to Home screen.
  • If you added it before v2.2, remove the old shortcut and add it again to get the new icon.
  • Your saved verses stay in that browser on that device. Use Export and Import to move them between devices.

Celebration Cheer

When you recite a verse correctly, the celebration screen plays a short cheer with a fanfare, applause, and crowd sounds along with the confetti. Use the Cheer: On / Off button on that screen to turn it off or on (the app remembers your choice). If you hear nothing on iPhone, check that the silent switch is off and the volume is up.

2. Taking a Quiz

  1. The app shows you a prompt (usually the reference, sometimes a starting hint) and asks you to type the verse from memory.
  2. Type what you remember and submit.
  3. If you get it right — great, move on to the next verse or round.
  4. If you get it wrong, the app will:
    • Show you the part of the verse you typed correctly.
    • Reveal just the next word you got wrong (not the whole verse).
    • Let you try again from there — so you keep testing your memory instead of just reading the answer.

3. Adjusting Hints

  • At any point, you can change "how full" your hints are — meaning how much of the verse is shown to you as a scaffold before you start typing.
  • Turn hints up when you're just learning a new verse.
  • Turn hints down as you get more confident, until you're typing the whole thing from a blank prompt.

4. Tips for Effective Memorization

  • Start with short verses and high hint levels, then gradually reduce hints as the verse sticks.
  • Practice the same verse across a few days rather than only once — repetition over time beats one long session.
  • Mix in review rounds of verses you've already learned so they don't fade.
  • If a verse has a tricky word order, use the "next wrong word" reveal as a natural way to drill the exact phrasing.

Recommended Starter Verses

Short, well-known verses are the easiest way to learn the app and build early wins:

Verse Reference Why it's a good starter
"Rejoice evermore." 1 Thessalonians 5:16 One of the shortest verses in the Bible — great for testing the mechanics
"Pray without ceasing." 1 Thessalonians 5:17 Also very short, pairs naturally with 5:16
"Jesus wept." John 11:35 The shortest verse in most English translations
"For God so loved the world..." John 3:16 Well-known, moderate length, good for practicing hint scaling
"I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me." Philippians 4:13 Popular, medium length, good rhythm for memorization
"The Lord is my shepherd; I shall not want." Psalm 23:1 Familiar opening line, easy to build on with the rest of Psalm 23
"Trust in the Lord with all thine heart..." Proverbs 3:5-6 Slightly longer — good once you're comfortable with the shorter ones
"Be still, and know that I am God." Psalm 46:10 Short, memorable, good for hint-level practice

Suggested order: start with 1 Thessalonians 5:16–17 and John 11:35 to learn how the app works, then move to John 3:16 or Psalm 23:1, and work up to longer verses like Proverbs 3:5-6 once you're comfortable turning hints down.
